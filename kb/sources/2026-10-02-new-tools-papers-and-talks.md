Honourable mentions: **time as a coordinate**, where positions, seasons and star age are pure functions of `t` (The Atlas; no seed change, effort S); a **deliberate outlier quota**, where about 5% of outputs are allowed to break the "natural" rules on purpose (Civ VII; changes seeds, S); **named rarity tiers** for spawn weights (Stellaris 4.0; S); **staged planet rolls behind a chosen class** (Shadow Empire; overlaps `constraints` and `presets`).

## 3. New feature keys

- `start-fairness`: start positions are balanced on purpose (spacing, no special systems in start areas, spread of habitable worlds).
- `contested-richness`: resource quality or richness raised by a system's position between starts (a distance-ratio score).
- `travel-terrain`: free movement changed by terrain (nebulae that slow, damage or hide; lanes as speed boosts) instead of, or as well as, a lane-only graph.
- `radial-progression`: difficulty, danger or material tier as a function of distance to a centre, optionally with barrier rings.
- `detail-levels`: the same seed rendered at several detail levels (minor bodies, belts, moons) without changing major bodies.
- `seed-search`: an API or tool that searches seed ranges for outputs matching a predicate.
- `fill-around-fixed`: the user pins some facts (a planet, a star, a profile code) and the generator fills in the rest to match them.
- `name-encodes-address`: an object's catalogue name decodes to its location or address and regenerates it.
- `culture-phonology`: names come from per-culture sound inventories and syllable rules, optionally drifting with distance or time.
- `constellations`: generated constellations and Bayer or Flamsteed-style star designations seen from a home system.
- `tag-filtered-text`: description grammar whose snippets are filtered by the object's facts or tags, so text cannot contradict data.
- `life-conditions-table`: life or biome placement by explicit condition rows (atmosphere, gravity, temperature, star class, climate layers).
- `landmark-guarantee`: each scale is guaranteed perceptually distinct features (landmark, monument, beacon), and the first outputs may be curated.
- `overlay-diffs`: user or player changes stored as a diff layer over the untouched generated base.
- `time-coordinate`: object state (orbital position, season, star age) computed as a pure function of a time value.
- `outlier-quota`: a set share of outputs may break the usual plausibility rules on purpose, for variety.
- `exo-demographics`: system architecture follows exoplanet population statistics (occurrence by star type, size and spacing patterns).

## 4. Exoplanet results as one-line game rules (2018 to 2025)

Each line is one rule a generator can apply. The numbers are rounded from the cited abstracts and summaries; treat them as defaults, not truths.

1. **Peas in a pod** (Weiss et al. 2018, AJ 155, 48): planets in one compact system are similar in size and regularly spaced; the outer planet of a pair is larger about 65% of the time.
2. **Spacing** (Weiss 2018): neighbours are usually about 20 mutual Hill radii apart; 93% of pairs are at least 10 apart. Use 10 as the stability floor and centre the draw on 20.
3. **Radius valley** (Fulton et al. 2017; reviewed in Cloutier 2024): few planets of 1.5 to 2.0 Earth radii. Draw small planets as rocky super-Earths (under 1.5) or gassy sub-Neptunes (2 to 4), rarely in between.
4. **Hot Neptune desert** (search summary of TESS demographics, 2026 arXiv 2601.09492; snippet): with periods under about 3 to 10 days, planets of 2 to 9 Earth radii are rare. Close-in worlds are either rocky or hot Jupiters.
5. **Hot Jupiters** (Kepler studies, search summary): about 0.5% of Sun-like stars (0.4% to 0.6%) have a giant with a period under 10 days.
6. **Cold giants** (Fulton et al. 2021, California Legacy Survey II): about 14 giant planets per 100 Sun-like stars at 2 to 8 au. Occurrence rises about 4 times near 1 au and peaks around 3 au, just past the frost line.
7. **Inner and outer link** (Zhu and Wu 2018; Bryan et al. 2019): given inner super-Earths, the chance of an outer cold giant is about 30% (29% to 34%), against about 10% overall. About 90% of cold-giant systems also have inner super-Earths.
8. **Star type** (search summary of Kepler M-dwarf studies, for example arXiv 2110.02971): M dwarfs have about 2 to 3 times more 1 to 3 Earth-radius planets inside 1 au than FGK stars, and giant planets are about 4 times rarer around them.
9. **Binaries** (Moe and Kratter 2021, MNRAS 507, 3593): companions inside 1 au leave no planets around either star (no S-type planets); at about 10 au the planet rate is about 15% of normal; past about 200 au there is no effect. About 43% of solar-type stars lack close planets for this reason.
10. **Earth-like in the habitable zone** (Bryson et al. 2021): conservatively 37% to 60% of Sun-like stars have a rocky planet in the habitable zone (up to 88% on optimistic bounds). That is a generous default for "a habitable-zone rocky world exists", not for "life exists".
11. **Metallicity** (classic result, Fischer and Valenti 2005; not re-checked here): giant planets become sharply more common around metal-rich stars. Tie giant odds to a galaxy-region metallicity value (feature key `galaxy-age-metallicity`).
