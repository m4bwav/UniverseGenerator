# Planet level design

Plan ideas P1 to P4, P6, P8 to P15, P17 and A7, worked out on 2026-10-02 before coding on branch `stage3-core`. Games and fiction first (D18): every formula is a cheap one that keeps the text and the numbers agreeing, checked against Earth, Venus, Mars and the Moon, not a simulation.

## The rule that keeps the other golden files still

A planet's existing fields (kind, zone, orbit, period, mass, radius, rings, moons, descriptor) are made exactly as before. Everything new is derived from them and from new streams of the planet's own seed (`Child(system, "planet", i)`, the seed of its address): `surface`, `atmosphere`, `rotation`, `life`, `traits`, `resources`, `anomaly`. No system-level stream gains or loses a draw, so `system.json` and `galaxy.json` stay byte for byte. Each stream draws a fixed number of values whatever it decides (determinism rule 6). Decisions are made on rounded values (temperature in whole kelvin, rain as an integer percentage), so a last-bit difference cannot flip one.

## Order of derivation

1. **Physics (P9), no draws.** Gravity M / R² (g), escape velocity 11.186 √(M / R) km/s, density 5.514 M / R³ g/cm³. Composition (P8) from the kind: Metal (iron), Rock (lava, barren, desert, rocky, greenhouse, garden), IceAndRock (ice, dwarf), Water (ocean), GasEnvelope (sub-Neptune), HydrogenHelium (gas giant, hot Jupiter), Ices (ice giant).
2. **Light and albedo (P10), stream `surface`.** Insolation S = L / a² (Earth 1; L summed for a close pair). Albedo by kind ± 0.05: lava 0.10, iron 0.12, barren 0.12, desert 0.28, rocky 0.22, greenhouse 0.75, ocean 0.30, garden 0.30, ice 0.60, dwarf 0.45, sub-Neptune 0.35, gas giant 0.34, ice giant 0.30, hot Jupiter 0.08. Equilibrium temperature T_eq = 278.6 (S (1 - A))^¼ (Earth 255 K). The same stream draws the water inventory h: ocean 0.92 to 1, garden 0.3 to 0.85, ice 0.6 to 1 (as ice), otherwise 0, and the garden and ocean target temperatures (below).
3. **Atmosphere by the Jeans rule (P11), stream `atmosphere`.** Exosphere temperature 4 T_eq; a gas of molecular mass μ stays when the escape velocity is at least 6 times its thermal speed, so the lightest gas kept is μ_min = 0.8976 × 4 T_eq / v_esc² (Earth 7.3: keeps water, nitrogen, oxygen, loses hydrogen and helium; Mars 30: loses nitrogen, keeps carbon dioxide). Stored as `Atmosphere.LightestGasKept`. By kind: giants and sub-Neptunes have an envelope (hydrogen, helium; ice giants add methane, sub-Neptunes water), no surface and a null pressure; barren, iron and dwarf worlds none; lava worlds a trace of rock vapour; desert 0.01 to 0.6 bar, rocky 0.05 to 3, greenhouse 20 to 150 (carbon dioxide first, sulphur dioxide clouds), ocean 0.5 to 8, garden 0.5 to 3 (nitrogen and oxygen), ice 40% a thin nitrogen and methane layer up to 1.5 bar, all log-uniform. Gases the planet cannot keep are dropped; if none is left the air is a trace "leaking away to space". The class follows the pressure (none 0, trace under 0.01, thin under 0.5, standard under 2, dense under 10, crushing from 10; envelope for giants). `Why` says in words what decided it. Breathable: oxygen present and 0.5 to 3 bar.
4. **Surface temperature, no draws.** A grey greenhouse: τ = k P^1.1, k 1.2 when carbon dioxide leads, 0.6 otherwise, plus 0.25 with open water; T = T_eq (1 + 0.75 τ)^¼. Earth 288 K, Venus about 790 K. Gardens and oceans start from a target temperature (garden 275 to 300 K, ocean 275 to 320 K), solve for the pressure that gives it, clamp the pressure to the kind's range and recompute, so a "temperate ocean world" is never boiling. Giants report the cloud-top temperature T_eq.
5. **Rotation, tilt, tidal lock (P12, A7), stream `rotation`.** Tidal lock radius 0.8 M*^⅓ au for 4.5 billion years (Gladman et al. 1996 form), × 0.85 young, × 1.1 old, × 0.35 for giants and sub-Neptunes. Inside 0.8 of it a planet is locked (rotation = its period), between 0.8 and 1 in a 3:2 resonance like Mercury, else free: rocky 8 to 60 hours (10% slow, 200 to 6,000 hours), giants 9 to 18, sub-Neptunes 10 to 40. Tilt 0 to 35° (sum of two uniforms), 10% 35 to 180° (over 90 spins backwards); locked planets a tenth of that. Day and night sides: redistribution r = P / (P + 0.1) (1 for giants); swing s = 1 - r, scaled by min(1, rotation / 100 h) for free spinners; day T (1 + 0.3 s), night T (1 - 0.7 s).
6. **Climate bands (P15), no draws.** Six bands of equal latitude (0 to 15° ... 75 to 90°) with their area shares; a band's temperature is T - D P2(sin φ), D = 0.43 T (1 - 0.8 r)(1 - tilt′ / 90), tilt′ folded into 0 to 90 (Earth: equator 300 K, pole 264 K). A locked planet's bands are measured from the point under its star (0 to 30° ... 140 to 180°, the 80 to 100° band is the terminator), at T + (day - night)(c - 0.417). Rain index per band: a Hadley pattern (wet equator, dry 15 to 30°, wet 45 to 60°, dry poles; for a locked world wet under the star) × humidity from h × a cold-air factor.
7. **Life (P3), stream `life`.** Ladder None, Prebiotic, Microbial, Simple, Complex; flora and fauna 0 to 5. Gardens are always Complex (flora 2 to 5, fauna 1 to 5, one less fauna in a young region). Oceans 15 / 30 / 30 / 25% none, microbial, simple, complex; rocky and desert worlds at 250 to 380 K and ice worlds may hold microbes or prebiotic chemistry; giants, sub-Neptunes and greenhouse worlds a 2 to 3% chance of microbes in the clouds; barren, iron, lava and dwarf worlds never (so the system tag "dead system" stays true).
8. **Biomes, no draws.** Each band splits into water (share h) and land. Water under 263 K is ice, otherwise ocean. Land by a Whittaker table on band temperature and rain when flora is at least 1 (tundra, taiga, temperate forest, grassland, savanna, rainforest, desert), and without plants bare rock or desert (ice under 263 K when there is water; lava worlds volcanic everywhere). Biome shares sum the bands; `Water` and `Ice` are the ocean and ice shares. Giants have no bands and no biomes.
9. **Traits and anomalies (P1), streams `traits` and `anomaly`.** One trait from a table of about 35, each with a condition on the data (dust storms on deserts, methane lakes only where methane is in the air, eyeball climate only on locked worlds with water, banded clouds on gas giants); a second at 40%. An anomaly ("gravity readings that do not match its mass") at the Weirdness percentage.
10. **Resources (P6), stream `resources`.** Metals, rare elements, ices, gases and organics graded 0 (none) or 1 to 5, a base by kind ± 1 (a zero stays zero), organics from life.
11. **Hazards (P4), no draws.** A list of hazards in words, each with a tier, and `Hazard` the worst: 1 shirt-sleeves, 2 a mask or coat (thin, heavy or unbreathable air, cold, heavy gravity, dust storms, wildlife), 3 a pressure suit (vacuum, deep cold, extreme heat, stellar flares, radiation), 4 heavy protection (crushing gravity, hard radiation), 5 lethal in minutes (no surface, crushing pressure, searing heat, molten rock).
12. **Habitability (P17), no draws.** `Similarity` is the Earth Similarity Index from radius, density, escape velocity and temperature (Schulze-Makuch et al. 2011, weights 0.57, 1.07, 0.70, 5.58). `Habitability` (Hostile, Marginal, Habitable, Ideal) is `HabitabilityFor(Species.Human)`; a `Species` record (temperature, gravity and pressure ranges, breathes oxygen) lets a game ask for its own peoples.
13. **Summary (P2), no draws.** A second line beside the descriptor, which stays as it is: "1 g, breathable air at 1.2 bar, 17 °C, 64% ocean, mostly temperate forest, complex life; hazard 1". Numbers are written from rounded integers, never through culture or runtime formatting.

## The lone planet

`Planet.Generate("my-seed")`, address `v1-my-seed/planet`, `v1-my-seed/planet/moon/0` for its moons. Its context comes from its own streams, as a lone system's does: age from `age`, a single star from `star` (the companion roll is ignored), the star's name from `names` with " b"; from `body` a target zone (hot 10, warm 15, temperate 35, cold 15, outer 25: games want temperate worlds), an orbit log-uniform inside that zone (never under three star radii; the zone is then read back from the orbit), and the size class, mass and kind by the system level's own `Draw`. Rings and moons, then the detail, come from the same streams as a planet in a system.

## Records

`Planet` gains Composition, Gravity, EscapeVelocity, Density, Insolation, Albedo, Temperature, DayTemperature, NightTemperature, Atmosphere (Class, Pressure, Gases, LightestGasKept, Breathable, Why), Water, Ice, Rotation, Spin (Free, Resonant, Locked), Tilt, Bands (From, To, Share, Temperature, Biome), Biomes (Biome, Share), Life, Flora, Fauna, Traits, Anomaly, Resources (Metals, RareElements, Ices, Gases, Organics), Hazard, Hazards, Similarity, Habitability and Summary. `tests/Golden/v1/planet.json` writes these explicitly (lone planets in full, planets of systems and galaxies by their new fields only). Moons keep their present fields; their detail is the next level (moon and belt detail).
